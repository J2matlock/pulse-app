# Subcontractor insurance & licenses: Never let an uninsured sub on your job site

*Business · starter `subcontractor-compliance`*

## The problem

Every sub owes you a current certificate of insurance, workers' comp, a license, a W-9 and a signed agreement, and every one of them runs out on its own date. The folder of PDFs doesn't warn you when the roofer's workers' comp lapsed last week, right before he starts on Monday.

## What Pulse sets up for you

For general contractors and builders: every subcontractor's certificates of insurance, workers' comp, licenses, W-9 and signed agreement, with 30-day warnings before anything runs out, and the subs on each job.

### Subcontractors

The trades you hire, with their insurance certificates, licenses and paperwork.

- Deadline columns: General liability expires, Workers' comp expires, Auto liability expires, Contractor license expires
- Deadline rule — Expires (30-day warning): the date entered is when it runs out; warns 30 days ahead
- Checklist: New sub onboarding
- Also: Trade, Contact person, Phone, Email, W-9 received, Agreement signed, Status, Notes

### Jobs

Projects and the subcontractors working on each one.

- Deadline columns: Start date, Finish date
- Deadline rule — Start date: the date entered is the start of the job; hidden once the job is done
- Deadline rule — Finish date: the date entered is when the job should be finished; hidden once it is done
- Linked to Subcontractors ("Subs on this job" here, "Jobs" there)
- Also: Address, Property owner, Status, Notes

### Also included

- Kinds of notes: Note, Call, Email, Certificate received
- Letters: Certificate of insurance request, Missing paperwork request
- Sample subcontractors to try it out, removed in one click when you're ready

## Your spreadsheet, but safer

- **Stays on your computer.** No cloud account and no upload. Your subcontractors never leave your machine.
- **Locked with your password.** Everything is encrypted, and Pulse locks itself when you step away.
- **Backed up for you.** Automatic backups, plus a recovery key in case you forget your password.
- **Colors that tell you what to do next.** Green is fine, yellow is coming up, red is overdue.
- **Bring what you have.** Import your existing spreadsheet from Excel or CSV.
