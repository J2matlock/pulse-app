# Representative payee: Annual accounting and monthly budgets for every beneficiary

*Legal and government · starter `representative-payee`*

## The problem

As a payee, you have an annual accounting due for each person, a monthly budget review, and benefit reviews that come up on their own schedule. With several beneficiaries, the dates and the paperwork are easy to mix up.

## What Pulse sets up for you

For organizational payees: the yearly accounting report, a monthly budget review for each beneficiary, rent and benefit reviews.

### Beneficiaries

People whose benefits you manage, with the yearly accounting and monthly budgets.

- Deadline columns: Annual accounting, Benefit review
- Deadline rule — Annual accounting: the yearly payee report, on the anniversary of becoming payee
- Deadline rule — Due date: the date entered is the deadline
- Budget review: 1 a month
- Also: Phone, Benefit, Monthly benefit, Payee since, Rent, Living situation

### Also included

- Kinds of notes: Budget review, Bill paid
- Sample beneficiaries to try it out, removed in one click when you're ready

## Your spreadsheet, but safer

- **Stays on your computer.** No cloud account and no upload. Your beneficiaries never leave your machine.
- **Locked with your password.** Everything is encrypted, and Pulse locks itself when you step away.
- **Backed up for you.** Automatic backups, plus a recovery key in case you forget your password.
- **Colors that tell you what to do next.** Green is fine, yellow is coming up, red is overdue.
- **Bring what you have.** Import your existing spreadsheet from Excel or CSV.

## Try it free

[Download Pulse](https://github.com/J2matlock/pulse-releases/releases/latest) for Windows or Linux and pick "Representative payee" when it asks how you want to start. The free version keeps up to 15 active beneficiaries; sample data never counts.
