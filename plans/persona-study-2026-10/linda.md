# Linda Marsh: "I am not retyping eighty people"

## 1. Who I am
Linda Marsh, 54, care coordinator at Bright Harbor for 22 years. I have 80 people on my caseload, a color-coded spreadsheet everyone borrows, a corrective-action note from Denise's last audit that still stings, and a greyhound named Biscuit asleep on my feet.
I use a mouse and I read every word. I never right-click and I don't use shortcuts. If a button is only a little picture, I don't know it's there.

Session: Wednesday at 7:00 p.m., one hour, plus a short look on Thursday morning. My file is `Bright Harbor caseload FINAL v3.xlsx`. It has a merged title row, then 80 people. Dates are typed three different ways, some cells say "TBD" or "see notes", there's a blank row halfway down, and the Notes are whole paragraphs.

(`09-summary.png`, `18-things-to-check.png`) *That gives me:* my evening back. Eighty people in about fifteen minutes and I didn't retype anybody.
- **"Change these" in the import, in plain words.** I set Med Review to every 3 months and it told me exactly when it would turn yellow. (`16-medreview-quarterly.png`) *That gives me:* the feeling that I'm setting up my own rules, not adopting someone else's.
- **Help answered my exact question and had a button to turn it on.** "2 contacts a month" led to the right answer and a "Turn on Requirements" button. (`42-help-answer.png`) *That gives me:* I didn't have to call my son.
- **The requirements screen speaks my language.** "1 face-to-face visit a month" was a ready choice, and it reads my rule back the way I'd say it. (`50-requirements-saved.png`) *That gives me:* the audit rule finally written down somewhere other than my head.

## 5. Suggestions
1. "A welcome box should never change my data. If it says 'Done', it should mean I'm done reading."
   → Change the first-look dialog's button to "Got it", and never mark anything done from a tour or hint.
2. "If I already have a Last Contact date this month, count it."
   → When Requirements are set up (or at import), offer "Count the dates already in Last Contact as one contact this month", picking which note kind they count as.
3. "When I log a visit or a call, update my Last Contact for me."
   → Add an option to link a date column to note kinds: logging a Visit, Phone or Email note sets Last Contact to the note's date.
4. "Ask me what my review dates mean, especially when some are in the future."
   → In the import's "Which dates come due again?", when a column has future dates, ask "Are these when it's due, or when it was last done?", and never show a future date as "Last done".
5. "Give me 'not this month' and keep my TBD people on the list."
   → Add relative date filters ("not in this month", "more than N days ago") with a checkbox to "include blank or not-a-date".
6. "Once I've set up requirements, put them at the top and show me the short list."
   → When Requirements is turned on, move its card above "What's due" and open it on "Behind and under 7 days left" (a short list), not 80 names in alphabetical order.
7. "Tell me whether a visit counts for both lines."
   → In the requirements dialog, add one line under the list: "A note counts toward every requirement it matches."
8. "Say whether that's people or deadlines."
   → Label them "Overdue people (36)" and "Overdue deadlines (38)".
9. "Let me read before I edit."
   → Clicking a long text value in Person details should expand it to read, with an "Edit" button, not select all the text.
10. "Point the bubbles at the thing they're talking about."
    → Anchor hints next to their target, not on top of it or on the neighboring panel, and don't show a hint while another popup is open.

## 6. Overall
Would I still be using Pulse in a week? **Yes, carefully.** I'll keep my spreadsheet open next to it for the rest of October and start logging every contact as a note so the requirements screen catches up by November. But I'd tell Denise to wait until I've fixed my review dates.

- **First impression: 8/10.** The password, the recovery key and "Bring in a spreadsheet" made me feel looked after from the first minute.
- **Ease of setup: 8/10.** My messy file came in whole in fifteen minutes, but the Done trap and the "last done in December" dates took points off.
- **Daily use: 5/10.** Two places to record every contact, and a requirements screen that starts at 0 of 80, make this more work than my spreadsheet for now.
- **Trust in my data: 6/10.** Encryption, backups and "(written Oct 15)" are wonderful, but a welcome box wrote a contact that never happened.
- **Would recommend to a coworker: 7/10.** "Try it, bring your spreadsheet, and don't click Done on the welcome box."
